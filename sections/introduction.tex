\section*{Introduction}

Microbial nutrient utilisation phenotypes are key determinants of ecological niches and influence organism distribution, interactions, and functional roles within complex ecosystems \cite{Green2008Biogeography,Malik2020Defining}.
A mechanistic understanding of these phenotypes informs community assembly, nutrient cycling, and biotechnological applications \cite{Falkowski2008-bx,Green2008Biogeography,Lu2014-zz}.
Traditional cultivation-based methods underlie most existing phenotype data but cannot characterise the metabolic capabilities of unculturable taxa \cite{Lloyd2018-ar}, introducing biases that restrict our view of microbial diversity.
Even for culturable organisms, comprehensive phenotypic characterisation across conditions remains resource-intensive \cite{Barberan2017-xi,Weimann2016Traitar}.

Advances in genomics offer a path to overcome these limitations \cite{Quince2017Shotgun,Nayfach2021Catalog}.
The growing availability of genomic data, including metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs), enables phenotype prediction directly from genomic information \cite{Nayfach2021Catalog,Li2023-up}, bypassing extensive laboratory experiments.
Although phylogeny has commonly served as a proxy for phenotype prediction, it is insufficient for novel organisms with limited phylogenetic context or for traits that are not phylogenetically conserved \cite{Li2023-up,Goberna2016Predicting}.
Genome-based predictions provide an alternative, enabling characterisation of both cultured and uncultured taxa while potentially uncovering the genetic mechanisms underlying phenotypic variation \cite{Weimann2016Traitar,Brbic2016Landscape,Karlsen2023Genotype,Lees2020Pangenome,Koblitz2025Phenotype}.
Such predictions leverage features from GC content \cite{Hu2022-fr} and genome size \cite{Li2019PredictiveGenomic,Chuckran2025-ap} to gene annotations and metabolic pathways \cite{Karlsen2023Genotype,Zampieri2019MLMetabolic}.

Machine learning models have become valuable tools for inferring phenotypes from complex genomic data, handling the nonlinear relationships and high dimensionality common in biological systems \cite{Greener2022-dy,Zampieri2019MLMetabolic,James2025OpenProblems}.
However, their performance and generalisability are constrained by data limitations and evaluation challenges \cite{Whalen2022Pitfalls,Shi2025OOD}.
Limited and taxonomically biased training data can lead to overfitting and poor applicability to diverse communities \cite{Whalen2022Pitfalls,Ramoneda2024-yb,Albright2023-ze}.
Phylogenetic correlations among genomic features further confound the identification of mechanistically relevant predictors \cite{Bradley2018-rs}; Li et al.\cite{Li2023-up} showed that taxonomically biased predictors primarily capture phylogenetic signal, motivating calls for larger and more diverse datasets \cite{Li2023-up,Koblitz2025Phenotype}.
This interpretation is well supported; however, an additional possibility is that mixed-quality genotype--phenotype mappings dilute the mechanistic signal available to the model \cite{Frenay2014LabelNoise,James2025OpenProblems}, making data coherence a constraint alongside sample size.
In contrast to prior strategies targeting feature-space artefacts \cite{Austin2025DEBIAS}, this possibility implicates the coherence of training labels themselves.
We therefore sought a system in which mechanistic expectations are strong, yet known representational and biological challenges remain.

We tested this hypothesis using microbial carbon utilisation as a model system.
Carbon utilisation phenotypes sit near the boundary of what genome-derived features can capture: the core biochemical transformations underlying many catabolic pathways are well characterised and conserved, yet their genomic realisation is often ambiguous because transporters and broad-specificity enzymes are difficult to annotate, divergent or uncharacterised genes can perform similar chemistry, and regulatory mechanisms are inconsistently conserved across phylogeny.
We integrated published phenotype records for 819 microbial genomes and their binary growth outcomes across 242 carbon sources from four independent experimental collections, then developed phenotype-specific classifiers and compared feature representations and evaluation strategies designed to test transfer across datasets and clades.

We find that models trained on concordant samples, defined as those with internally consistent genotype--phenotype mappings, act as specialist predictors: they show stronger cross-dataset generalisation, more often recover established pathway genes as top predictive features, and correctly classify a subset of samples misclassified by the mechanistic predictor.
For histidine utilisation, 50--100 high-quality training samples were sufficient for strong performance across evaluation settings, consistent with the hypothesis that data coherence, rather than dataset size alone, is a key constraint on phenotype prediction.
Model prediction confidence further provided a label-free ranking signal for genomes whose concordance is unknown, and prioritising low-confidence genomes for additional measurement improved generalisation, indicating that data curation and applicability-domain definition jointly determine when genome-based phenotype predictions are useful.
