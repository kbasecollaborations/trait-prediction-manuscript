\section*{Introduction}

Microbial nutrient utilisation phenotypes are key determinants of ecological niches and underpin community assembly, nutrient cycling, and biotechnological applications~\cite{Green2008Biogeography,Malik2020Defining,Falkowski2008-bx,Lu2014-zz}.
Cultivation-based methods underlie most phenotype data but cannot characterise unculturable taxa and remain resource-intensive even for culturable organisms~\cite{Lloyd2018-ar,Barberan2017-xi}.

Advances in genomics offer a path to overcome these limitations \cite{Quince2017Shotgun,Nayfach2021Catalog}, enabling phenotype prediction directly from genomic information, including from metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs) \cite{Nayfach2021Catalog,Li2023-up}, and bypassing extensive laboratory experiments.
Although phylogeny has commonly served as a proxy for phenotype prediction, it is insufficient for novel organisms with limited phylogenetic context or for traits that are not phylogenetically conserved \cite{Li2023-up,Goberna2016Predicting}.
Genome-based predictions provide an alternative, enabling characterisation of both cultured and uncultured taxa while potentially uncovering the genetic mechanisms underlying phenotypic variation \cite{Weimann2016Traitar,Brbic2016Landscape,Karlsen2023Genotype,Lees2020Pangenome,Koblitz2025Phenotype}.

Machine learning models have become valuable tools for inferring phenotypes from complex genomic data, handling the nonlinear relationships and high dimensionality common in biological systems \cite{Greener2022-dy,Zampieri2019MLMetabolic,James2025OpenProblems}.
However, their performance and generalisability are constrained by data limitations and evaluation challenges \cite{Whalen2022Pitfalls,Shi2025OOD}.
Limited and taxonomically biased training data can lead to overfitting and poor applicability to diverse communities \cite{Whalen2022Pitfalls,Ramoneda2024-yb,Albright2023-ze}.
Phylogenetic correlations among genomic features further confound the identification of mechanistically relevant predictors \cite{Bradley2018-rs}; Li et al.\cite{Li2023-up} showed that taxonomically biased predictors primarily capture phylogenetic signal, motivating calls for larger and more diverse datasets \cite{Li2023-up,Koblitz2025Phenotype}.
This interpretation is well supported; however, an additional possibility is that mixed-quality genotype--phenotype mappings dilute the mechanistic signal available to the model \cite{Frenay2014LabelNoise,James2025OpenProblems}, implicating the coherence of training labels themselves rather than feature-space artefacts \cite{Austin2025DEBIAS} as a constraint alongside sample size.

We tested this hypothesis using microbial carbon utilisation as a model system, a domain in which mechanistic expectations are strong yet known representational and biological challenges remain.
Carbon utilisation phenotypes sit near the boundary of what genome-derived features can capture: the core biochemical transformations underlying many catabolic pathways are well characterised and conserved, yet their genomic realisation is often ambiguous because transporters and broad-specificity enzymes are difficult to annotate, divergent or uncharacterised genes can perform similar chemistry, and regulatory mechanisms are inconsistently conserved across phylogeny.
We integrated published phenotype records for 819 microbial genomes and their binary growth outcomes across 242 carbon sources from four independent experimental collections, then developed phenotype-specific classifiers and compared feature representations and evaluation strategies designed to test transfer across datasets and clades.

We find that models trained on concordant samples, defined as those with internally consistent genotype--phenotype mappings, act as specialist predictors: they generalise better across datasets, more often recover established pathway genes as top features, and correctly classify a subset of samples misclassified by the mechanistic predictor.
Most phenotypes saturated at 50--100 coherent training samples, and per-genome prediction confidence provided a label-free ranking signal for genomes whose concordance is unknown, supporting the view that data coherence and applicability-domain definition jointly determine when genome-based phenotype predictions are useful.
