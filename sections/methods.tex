\section*{Materials and Methods}

\subsection*{Carbon Source Utilisation Datasets}

We obtained four published binary growth datasets on defined carbon sources: ATLeaf~\cite{Schafer2023-nk}, Biolog~\cite{BiologDatasetCitation}, Marine~\cite{Gralka2023-ft}, and Populus~\cite{Timm2015Populus} (per-dataset strain and substrate counts in Supplementary Text~S1 and Table~\ref{tab:dataset_overview}).
% TODO(CITATION): Ask Chris Henry for the correct source citation for the Biolog phenotype data
These contributed 819 input genomes, of which 795 were retained after quality control combining assembly-fragmentation, CheckM2 v1.1.0, feature-profile outlier, and pangenome-completeness filters (Supplementary Text~S2; Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:pangenome_completeness}).
Carbon source names were standardised and analyses were restricted to the 15 phenotypes shared across all four datasets.

\subsection*{Genome Annotation}

Each genome was annotated with three tools.
KofamScan v1.3.0 \cite{Aramaki2020KofamKOALA} assigned KEGG Orthologs via profile HMMs from the KEGG database \cite{Kanehisa2000KEGG}.
GapMind for carbon sources~\cite{Price2022GapMindCarbon,Price2020GapMind} predicted carbon-utilisation pathway completeness, scored 0 (incomplete) to 2 (high confidence).
RAST \cite{Aziz2008RAST} provided functional predictions, parsed to extract subsystem ontology terms.

\subsection*{Phylogenetic Tree Construction}

We assigned taxonomy with GTDB-Tk \cite{Chaumeil2022GTDBTk} (GTDB release v220) and placed genomes into the GTDB \cite{Parks2018GTDB} reference tree using \texttt{classify\_wf}.
We extracted the corresponding subtree and computed distances between tips with ETE 3 \cite{HuertaCepas2016ETE3}.
Genomes lacking species-level assignments were placed at genus or family level.

\subsection*{Baseline Predictors}

GapMind Mechanistic Predictions: We evaluated GapMind as a mechanistic baseline using two binary thresholds on pathway-completeness calls: a strict threshold (only "complete" pathways positive) and a permissive threshold ("complete" and "likely complete" positive); all other calls were negative (Supplementary Text~S3). Phylogeny-Based Nearest Neighbour: A phylogenetic nearest-neighbour classifier assigned phenotypes by majority vote among the $k=3$ closest training relatives. Null Models: Null baselines comprised an identity model (predicting the most common training class) and a Bernoulli model (sampling from observed training class probabilities).

\subsection*{Data Pre-processing and Feature Selection}

KOFAM and GapMind annotations were converted into binary presence/absence matrices (GapMind pathway scores retained integer values).
Two pre-processing filters were applied to training data only: variance filtering (scikit-learn \cite{Pedregosa2011Sklearn} VarianceThreshold at 0.01) and correlation filtering (Spearman $\rho > 0.95$, graph-based, retaining the most connected features).
KOFAM yielded 4000--5000 features per dataset and GapMind around 1000.

\subsection*{Machine Learning Classifiers}

We trained gradient-boosted tree classifiers using CatBoost~\cite{Prokhorenkova2018CatBoost} (v1.2.8), which outperformed random forests in most train--test and feature combinations (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:best_models_counts}; Supplementary Text~S3).
The hyperparameters of the CatBoost model were tuned based on performance across all phenotypes: 1000 iterations, learning rate 0.03, tree depth 4, L2 regularization 15, random subspace method (10\% features per split), Bayesian bootstrap and early stopping in logloss validation with patience 50.
Random holdout evaluations used five 70:15:15 train/validation/test partitions stratified by phenotype cluster (genomes sharing the same binary growth pattern across the 15 phenotypes), preserving the joint phenotype distribution; data-split random states were set to 42--46 (Figure~\ref{fig:ml_performance}A).
Cross-dataset and phylogenetic evaluations used a single 85:15 train:validation partition on the non-test data.
The CatBoost training \texttt{random\_state} was fixed to 42, with SHAP stability analyses sweeping it across 20 seeds.

\subsection*{Evaluation Strategies}

\subsubsection*{Random Holdout and Cross-Dataset Testing}

Performance was evaluated under four scenarios: random holdout within the aggregated dataset; leave-one-dataset-out cross-dataset; and in-clade and out-of-clade phylogenetic splits.
Phenotype--dataset combinations with fewer than 10 minority-class test samples were excluded to prevent balanced-accuracy collapse; cross-dataset missing features were imputed as zero (Supplementary Text~S3).

\subsubsection*{Phylogenetic Partitioning}

We applied the out-of-clade partitioning algorithm of \cite{Li2023-up}, which selects phylogenetically separated test clades (12--18\% of samples).

\subsection*{Concordant and Discordant Sample Analysis}

Samples were stratified by agreement between GapMind permissive-threshold predictions and experimental outcomes: concordant samples (GapMind matches the experiment) and discordant samples (GapMind contradicts it); 71.5\% of labelled samples were concordant across the 15 phenotypes and four datasets.
Models trained on concordant samples only were evaluated on both subsets.
A pipeline-ceiling reference (Figure~\ref{fig:concordant_analysis}A, red dashed lines) repeated the procedure with raw GapMind step features (Supplementary Text~S5).

\subsection*{Feature Importance Analysis}

Stable predictive features were identified by SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations)~\cite{Lundberg2017SHAP} analysis of a fixed 500-iteration CatBoost variant trained without early stopping.
For each phenotype--dataset configuration, models were trained across 20 random seeds; the top 10 features by mean absolute SHAP value per run were extracted, and features appearing in $\geq$70\% of runs were designated ``stable''.
To avoid counting biologically redundant KOs (paralogues, operon-mates, alternative enzymes) as separate features, per-phenotype stable KOs were grouped into SHAP-supervised redundancy clusters via \texttt{shap.utils.hclust}~\cite{Lundberg2017SHAP,Lundberg2020TreeSHAP} on the pooled concordant feature matrix at distance threshold 0.5; the resulting KO-to-cluster mapping replaced raw KO identity for intersection and uniqueness counts (Figures~\ref{fig:misclassification_diagnosis}C and~\ref{fig:concordant_analysis}B; Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:feature_comparison} and~\ref{tab:feature_comparison_concordant}; Supplementary Text~S8).
This pooled clustering was used only for post hoc interpretive grouping and did not affect training, predictions, or reported metrics.
For Table~\ref{tab:main_feature_comparison}, cluster representatives were mapped to KEGG reference pathway maps) instead of stricter catabolism modules, providing a uniform coverage metric across all 15 phenotypes.

\subsection*{Data Quality Filtering Strategies}

Two additional filtering strategies were evaluated beyond concordant sample filtering.
\emph{Problematic-sample removal} excluded microbes with zero growth on all substrates despite a GapMind growth prediction, and microbes with growth on all substrates despite incomplete GapMind pathways, applied only during training-set assessment, never at inference.
\emph{Confidence-based filtering} excluded ambiguous training samples by a composite score (phylogenetic neighbour agreement, GapMind pathway completeness, experimental outcome), applied only during training-set assessment, never at inference; weights and thresholds are in Supplementary Text~S10.
Under both strategies, the same full cross-dataset held-out test set was used for evaluation, so the bars in Figure~\ref{fig:improvement_strategies}B are directly comparable across conditions.

\subsection*{Sample Size Requirements}

Minimum training data requirements were assessed via learning curves: for each phenotype, models were trained on stratified subsets of 50, 100, 200, 500, and full training samples (three repeats per configuration) and evaluated across random holdout, cross-dataset, and out-of-clade scenarios on full, concordant, and discordant test subsets.

\subsection*{Reliability and Experiment-Prioritisation Analyses}

For full-data and concordant-trained models on the cross-dataset test set, prediction confidence was defined as $\max(p,\,1-p)$ and used as a ranking score; calibration was reported separately via reliability diagrams and expected calibration error.
Confidence ROC AUC for distinguishing correct from incorrect predictions was computed per phenotype and pooled using \texttt{sklearn.metrics.roc\_auc\_score} \cite{Pedregosa2011Sklearn}.
Risk--coverage curves used class-stratified abstention to avoid degenerate single-class retained subsets (Supplementary Text~S9), and label-free aggregates (mean confidence, fraction of high-confidence predictions, feature-space novelty) were Spearman-correlated with per-phenotype cross-dataset balanced accuracy.

\subsection*{Statistical Analysis}

Paired comparisons of balanced accuracy across phenotypes used two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on phenotype-level means (\texttt{scipy.stats.wilcoxon}); Spearman rank correlations linked per-phenotype label-free aggregates to cross-dataset balanced accuracy (\texttt{scipy.stats.spearmanr}).
The ten paired tests reported (eight Wilcoxon, two Spearman) were jointly Benjamini--Hochberg adjusted at FDR $\leq 0.05$ (\texttt{scripts/stats/manuscript\_pvalues.py}); raw $p$-values are reported in the main text with adjusted $q$-values inline for borderline tests.
Summary statistics are mean $\pm$ standard deviation across folds or repeats unless otherwise noted.
