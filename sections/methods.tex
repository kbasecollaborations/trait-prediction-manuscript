\section*{Materials and Methods}

\subsection*{Carbon Source Utilisation Datasets}

We obtained four published binary growth datasets describing bacterial growth on defined carbon sources.
ATLeaf \cite{Schafer2023-nk} comprised 224 strains tested on 45 carbon substrates using minimal media on agar plates.
Biolog encompassed 362 strains across 64 substrates, compiled from published studies using Biolog Phenotype MicroArray technology \cite{Bochner2001PM,Bochner2009PM}.
Marine \cite{Gralka2023-ft} contained 175 marine heterotrophic bacteria assessed across 100 carbon substrates.
Populus~\cite{Timm2015Populus} included 58 bacterial isolates tested on 191 carbon sources.

Annotation and phenotype completeness reduced the final analysis sets (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:dataset_overview}).
Genome quality control combined assembly-fragmentation, CheckM2, feature-profile outlier, and pangenome-completeness filters (Supplementary Text~S2; Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:pangenome_completeness}).
We standardised carbon source names, converted the source records into binary phenotype matrices suitable for machine-learning analyses, and restricted analyses to the 15 phenotypes shared across the four datasets, used jointly for aggregated analyses and leave-one-dataset-out cross-dataset evaluations.

\subsection*{Genome Annotation}

Each genome was annotated with three tools.
RAST \cite{Aziz2008RAST} provided functional predictions, parsed to extract subsystem ontology terms.
KofamScan \cite{Aramaki2020KofamKOALA} assigned KEGG Orthologs via profile HMMs from the KEGG database \cite{Kanehisa2000KEGG}, run per genome in parallel.
GapMind~\cite{Price2020GapMind} predicted carbon-utilisation pathway completeness, with scores ranging from 0 (incomplete) to 2 (high confidence).

\subsection*{Phylogenetic Tree Construction}

We assigned taxonomy with GTDB-Tk \cite{Chaumeil2022GTDBTk}
\ (v2.x, GTDB R214 reference) and placed genomes into the GTDB \cite{Parks2018GTDB} reference tree using the \texttt{classify\_wf} workflow.
For analyses requiring a single tree across all genomes, we extracted the corresponding subtree and computed cophenetic distances between tips with the ETE 3 toolkit \cite{HuertaCepas2016ETE3}.
Genomes lacking species-level assignments were placed at the genus or family level and flagged in the supplementary metadata.

\subsection*{Baseline Predictors}

\subsubsection*{GapMind Mechanistic Predictions}

We evaluated GapMind as a mechanistic baseline using two binary thresholds on pathway-completeness calls.
The strict threshold treated only \texttt{complete} pathways as positive predictions; the permissive threshold treated \texttt{complete} and \texttt{likely\_complete} pathways as positive predictions.
All other GapMind calls (\texttt{steps\_missing\_low}, \texttt{steps\_missing\_medium}, \texttt{steps\_missing\_high}, \texttt{incomplete}, and \texttt{not\_present}) were treated as negative predictions.

\subsubsection*{Phylogeny-Based Nearest Neighbor}

A phylogenetic nearest-neighbor classifier assigned phenotypes by majority vote among the $k=3$ closest training relatives.

\subsubsection*{Null Models}

Null baselines comprised an identity model (predicting the most common training class) and a Bernoulli model (sampling from observed training class probabilities).

\subsection*{Data Pre-processing and Feature Selection}

KOFAM and GapMind annotations were converted into binary presence/absence matrices (GapMind pathway scores retained numeric values).
Two filtering steps were applied to the training data only, to prevent leakage:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Variance filtering: removed features with variance below 0.01 using scikit-learn's \cite{Pedregosa2011Sklearn} VarianceThreshold.
    \item Correlation filtering: removed highly correlated features (Spearman $\rho > 0.95$) via a graph-based approach retaining the most connected features.
\end{itemize}
After filtering, KOFAM yielded approximately 4000--5000 features per dataset and GapMind approximately 1000.

\subsection*{Machine Learning Classifiers}

We trained gradient-boosted tree classifiers using CatBoost \cite{Prokhorenkova2018CatBoost}, which outperformed random forests in most train--test dataset and feature combinations (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:best_models_counts}).
CatBoost was configured with 1000 iterations, learning rate 0.03, tree depth 4, L2 regularisation 15, random subspace method (10\% features per split), Bayesian bootstrap, and early stopping with a patience of 50 iterations.
Repeated random holdout evaluations used five phenotype-cluster-based 70:15:15 train:validation:test partitions generated with random states 42--46; cross-dataset and phylogenetic evaluations used 85:15 train:validation partitions on the non-test data.
Early stopping was based on validation Logloss, and balanced accuracy was the primary reporting metric to account for class imbalance.
Hyperparameters were not tuned per phenotype; reported performance reflects the above settings with \texttt{random\_state=42}, except for SHAP stability analyses (Fig.~\ref{fig:misclassification_diagnosis}C, Fig.~\ref{fig:concordant_analysis}B), which used 20 seeds (see below).

\subsection*{Evaluation Strategies}

\subsubsection*{Random Holdout and Cross-Dataset Testing}

Performance was evaluated under four scenarios: (i) repeated random holdout evaluation within the aggregated dataset; (ii) cross-dataset evaluation (training on three datasets, testing on the held-out fourth); (iii) in-clade cross-dataset evaluation restricted to phylogenetically similar samples; and (iv) out-of-clade evaluation on phylogenetically distant samples.
For all cross-dataset and phylogenetic evaluations, phenotype--dataset combinations with fewer than 10 samples in the minority class of the held-out test set were excluded at aggregation time; this rule prevents the balanced-accuracy metric from collapsing on test cells with extreme class imbalance (e.g., 36--37 growers and one non-grower in the Populus concordant test set for some amino acids).
The minority count was computed against the appropriate test-set definition for each figure---the concordant subset for Figure~\ref{fig:concordant_analysis}A, the discordant subset for Figure~\ref{fig:concordant_analysis}C, and the full labelled subset for Figures~\ref{fig:ml_performance}B, \ref{fig:improvement_strategies}B--D, and~\ref{fig:full_held_out_test}.
Implementation is consolidated in \texttt{scripts/minority\_filter.py}.
For cross-dataset testing, features were mapped from training to test data with missing features imputed as zero; this conflates true gene absence with annotation mismatch and may underestimate cross-dataset performance.

\subsubsection*{Phylogenetic Partitioning}

We applied the out-of-clade partitioning algorithm of \cite{Li2023-up}, which selects phylogenetically separated test clades within a target size range (12--18\% of samples), supplemented by manual class-level partitioning using Gammaproteobacteria and Alphaproteobacteria as out-of-clade test cases.

\subsection*{Concordant and Discordant Sample Analysis}

Samples were stratified by agreement between GapMind predictions and experimental outcomes: concordant (GapMind matches the experiment) and discordant (GapMind contradicts it).
Concordance was defined throughout using the permissive GapMind threshold described above.
Models were trained on concordant samples only and evaluated on both concordant and discordant test sets.
Per-phenotype, per-dataset counts of concordant and discordant samples (and exclusions) are reported in Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:concordance_counts}.
As a pipeline-ceiling reference for the KOFAM concordant analysis (Figure~\ref{fig:concordant_analysis}A, red dashed lines), the same concordant-training, cross-dataset-evaluation procedure was repeated with the raw (unfiltered) GapMind step features in place of KOFAM features.
Raw rather than correlation-filtered GapMind features were used here because GapMind represents the same underlying gene under multiple per-phenotype prefixes (for example, the same amino-acid transporter appears as \texttt{Alanine-braC}, \texttt{Serine-braC}, etc.), so the standard 0.95 correlation filter consolidates these duplicates and unfairly depletes the per-phenotype feature space for some pathways.
Because concordant labels are by construction consistent with GapMind's pathway-completeness rule, the GapMind-feature setting establishes the balanced accuracy reachable by the pipeline when feature resolution matches the mechanism; the per-phenotype gap between this reference and the KOFAM result quantifies pathway-annotation information that is present in GapMind but absent or fragmented in KOFAM.

\subsection*{Feature Importance Analysis}

Stable predictive features were identified using SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) analysis \cite{Lundberg2017SHAP}.
SHAP analyses used a fixed 500-iteration CatBoost variant without early stopping (to avoid validation-set dependence).
For each phenotype--dataset configuration, models were trained across 20 random seeds, extracting the top 10 features by mean absolute SHAP value per run.
Features appearing in at least 70\% of runs were designated ``stable features'' and compared across datasets.
For the high-dimensional KOFAM concordant analysis in Figure~\ref{fig:concordant_analysis}B, each configuration was first screened to the top 300 KOFAM features by CatBoost PredictionValuesChange importance, with SHAP stability analysis then performed on the screened matrix.

To prevent biologically redundant KOs (paralogues, operon-mates, alternative enzymes in the same pathway) from being counted as separate stable features, the union of stable KOs identified for each phenotype was further grouped into target-aware redundancy clusters using the \texttt{shap.utils.hclust} routine of the SHAP package \cite{Lundberg2017SHAP,Lundberg2020TreeSHAP}.
This procedure fits a univariate gradient-boosted model from each candidate KO to the phenotype and computes a pairwise distance of $1-R^{2}$ between the resulting predictions; two KOs are placed in the same cluster when they share more than 50\% of their univariate explanatory power with respect to the phenotype (single linkage, default cut-off of 0.5).
Clustering was performed on the pooled concordant feature matrix across all four datasets so that cluster identities are common to the full-data and concordant-data analyses, and the resulting per-phenotype KO-to-cluster mapping replaces raw KO identity for all intersection and uniqueness counts (Figures~\ref{fig:misclassification_diagnosis}C and~\ref{fig:concordant_analysis}B; Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:feature_comparison} and~\ref{tab:feature_comparison_concordant}).
For three phenotypes (alanine, mannose, serine) the stable features picked by different concordant-trained models did not reach this target-redundancy threshold yet remained cross-correlated within the held-out dataset (e.g., for mannose, the ribose- and xylose-uptake transporter ATP-binding proteins K10441 and K10545 with $|r|=0.67$ in the held-out concordant ATLeaf samples).
Inspection of the underlying KOFAM annotations showed these features comprise a heterogeneous mixture of carbohydrate-utilisation and accessory genes rather than members of a shared mechanistic pathway, indicating that for these three phenotypes the concordant-trained model generalises through co-occurring substrate-utilisation markers characteristic of broad carbohydrate utilisers rather than through convergent mechanistic features --- a less specific mode of transfer that remains within the applicability domain of organisms sharing these broader genomic repertoires.

\subsection*{Data Quality Filtering Strategies}

Two additional filtering strategies were evaluated beyond concordant sample filtering.

\subsubsection*{Problematic Sample Identification}

Problematic samples were identified in two categories: microbes with zero growth on all substrates despite at least one GapMind growth prediction, and microbes with growth on all substrates despite incomplete GapMind pathways.
Inspection of the 20 genomes with the highest GapMind misclassification count across the 15 phenotypes showed most belonged to one of these categories.
\subsubsection*{Confidence-Based Filtering}

A composite confidence score was defined from three inputs:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Phylogenetic confidence ($w=0.2$): agreement among $k=3$ nearest phylogenetic neighbours.
    \item GapMind confidence ($w=0.3$): pathway completeness score from GapMind.
    \item Experimental data ($w=0.5$): binary experimental outcome.
\end{itemize}
The experimental-outcome component is used solely for training-set quality assessment and sample exclusion; it is never available to the model at inference, since feature matrices supplied to the classifier do not include the phenotype label.
GapMind confidence was mapped to a six-level scale (0.0--1.0) based on pathway completeness categories.
Samples with composite scores between 0.4 and 0.6 were considered ambiguous and excluded from filtered training sets.
\subsection*{Sample Size Requirements}

Minimum training data requirements were assessed using learning curves.
For each phenotype, models were trained on stratified subsets of 50, 100, 200, 500, and full training samples with three repeats per configuration.
Performance was evaluated across random holdout, cross-dataset, and out-of-clade evaluations, and on full, concordant, and discordant test subsets.

\subsection*{Reliability and Experiment-Prioritisation Analyses}

For both the full-data and concordant-trained models evaluated on the held-out cross-dataset test set, prediction confidence was defined as $\max(p,\,1-p)$ from the predicted class probabilities.
These CatBoost probabilities were not post hoc calibrated, so confidence was used only as a ranking score for selective prediction rather than as an absolute probability of correctness.
Calibration was assessed with reliability diagrams and the expected calibration error over ten equal-width probability bins, pooled across phenotypes for each model.
Per-phenotype risk--coverage curves were generated by stratifying abstention by predicted class: within each phenotype and model, predictions were partitioned by the predicted label and at each coverage level the top fraction of most-confident class-1 predictions (highest $p$) and the top fraction of most-confident class-0 predictions (highest $1-p$) were retained in parallel before balanced accuracy was recomputed on the retained subset.
This class-stratified construction avoids the degenerate single-predicted-class subsets that arise on individual phenotypes when ranking by raw $\max(p,\,1-p)$ for class-skewed classifiers, which would collapse balanced accuracy on the retained subset to 0.5 regardless of accuracy on the discarded fraction.
Feature-space novelty of a held-out genome was defined as its mean Jaccard distance to the five nearest training genomes in KOFAM feature space, computed against the appropriate per-split training set.
To test whether reliability could be anticipated per phenotype, label-free aggregates (mean confidence, fraction of high-confidence predictions, and mean feature-space novelty) were correlated with each phenotype's cross-dataset balanced accuracy across the 15 phenotypes using Spearman correlation.
For the retrospective experiment-prioritisation simulation, each held-out dataset was split into candidate and evaluation pools; 25 candidate genome labels were added to the concordant training set and the model retrained, measuring the change in cross-dataset balanced accuracy on the evaluation pool across three random seeds and the four held-out datasets.
Candidate genomes were selected by four label-free strategies---lowest model confidence, highest feature-space novelty, feature-space diversity, and random---and strategies were compared with the two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test on paired runs.
Models in this simulation were trained with a reduced number of boosting iterations for computational efficiency.

\subsection*{Statistical Analysis}

Paired comparisons of balanced accuracy across phenotypes (model versus baseline; combined versus phenotype-filtered features; random versus cross-dataset splits) used the two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test on phenotype-level mean balanced accuracies, computed with \texttt{scipy.stats.wilcoxon}.
P-values are uncorrected and are reported as descriptive summaries of paired differences rather than confirmatory inferences.
Summary statistics are reported as mean $\pm$ standard deviation across folds or repeats unless otherwise noted.
