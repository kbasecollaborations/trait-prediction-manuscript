\begin{abstract}
{
\noindent
The carbon sources a microorganism can utilise are key determinants of its ecological niche, yet are difficult to predict from genomes even for well-characterised pathways.
Machine-learning models often achieve high within-dataset accuracy but generalise poorly across datasets, and whether this reflects the algorithm or the training data remains unresolved.
To distinguish these explanations, we integrated binary growth phenotypes for 819 strains across 242 carbon sources from four datasets, focusing on the 15 shared sources.
Cross-dataset accuracy declined even among close relatives, so phylogeny alone did not explain it.
Restricting training to concordant strains (measured growth matching their annotated pathways), under an identical pipeline, increased the carbon sources with predictive pathway genes recovered across datasets from six to twelve of fifteen, whereas matched random subsets did not.
A larger literature-curated collection (BacDive) improved the unfiltered model but did not significantly exceed this concordant set.
Because concordance filtering requires a mechanistic predictor, a mechanism-free filter based on phylogenetic agreement and experimental labels recovered part of the improvement but not the gain in recall.
Neither surpassed a curated pathway-based predictor overall; instead, the concordance-trained model acted as a bounded specialist, recovering growth missed by incomplete annotations more often than it overturned annotated pathways lacking growth (44\% versus 19\%), predominantly among metabolic generalists, not substrate-specific routes.
Generalisation therefore depends less on training-set size than on label agreement with annotated pathways, and prioritising low-confidence genomes for measurement improved it more than random or diversity-based selection.
}
\end{abstract}

\keywords{Microbial ecology, Comparative genomics, Machine learning, Genotype-phenotype mapping, Cross-dataset generalisation}
