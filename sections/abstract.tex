\begin{abstract}
  {
    \noindent
Predicting microbial nutrient utilization from genomic data is crucial yet challenging due to complex regulatory interactions and incomplete mechanistic understanding.
Current mechanistic models are limited by incomplete annotations and unknown pathways, while phylogenetic inference, though commonly used, is restricted to evolutionary proximity and fails to generalize across distant contexts.
To investigate these limitations, we employed a mechanism-agnostic machine learning approach to predict microbial growth across 242 carbon sources using a comprehensive dataset of 819 microbial genomes from four independent collections.
While this approach enables phenotype prediction, feature importance analysis revealed that models trained on complete datasets frequently identified phylogenetically correlated features rather than mechanistically relevant genes.

Our dataset integrated genomic annotations from multiple sources including metabolic pathway features (RAST, KOFAM) and protein functional data (UniProt), and we developed phenotype-specific classifiers using gradient-boosted decision trees.
Cross-dataset prediction accuracy was significantly lower than intra-dataset accuracy, a discrepancy we analyzed by systematically disentangling dataset-specific biases from phylogenetic distance.
Integrating multiple annotation sources improved performance for 82\% of phenotypes; however, neither improved annotation coverage nor biologically informed feature selection resolved generalizability failures.

Crucially, when we restricted training to samples where mechanism-based predictions (GapMind) matched experimental outcomes, models achieved near-perfect accuracy and robust generalizability across datasets and clades.
This revealed that poor generalizability stems primarily from incomplete genome annotations, experimental measurement variability, and genuine biological pathway differences rather than algorithmic inadequacy.
Our findings demonstrate that under controlled conditions with high-quality data, machine learning successfully identifies mechanistically relevant genomic features, emphasizing that future improvements must focus on data quality and determining optimal confidence thresholds rather than model architecture alone.
  }
\end{abstract}
