\begin{abstract}
  {
    \noindent
Predicting microbial carbon utilization from genomic data remains challenging despite well-characterized metabolic pathways.
Mechanistic approaches achieve only modest accuracy ($\sim$0.65--0.70 balanced accuracy), while phylogeny-based methods fail to generalize beyond training distributions.
Here, we compiled growth phenotypes for 819 microbial genomes across 242 carbon sources from four independent experimental collections and developed phenotype-specific classifiers to systematically diagnose generalization failures.

Machine learning models achieved strong within-dataset accuracy (0.80--0.90) but exhibited substantially reduced cross-dataset and out-of-clade performance (drops of 0.15--0.25).
Feature importance analysis revealed that models trained on complete datasets identified phylogenetically correlated rather than mechanistically relevant genes, with different datasets yielding almost entirely different top features for identical phenotypes.
This instability explains why high within-dataset accuracy fails to translate to robust generalization.

Critically, when we restricted training to samples where mechanistic predictions (GapMind) correctly matched experimental outcomes, models achieved near-perfect accuracy (>0.95) that generalized robustly across datasets, phylogenetic clades, and evaluation scenarios.
These models consistently identified established pathway genes as top features.
Systematic characterization of prediction discordances revealed that models trained on concordant data could partially predict discordant samples (balanced accuracy 0.18--0.4), suggesting that discordances arise from a mixture of annotation gaps, regulatory complexity, and measurement variability.

Our findings establish that generalization failures in microbial phenotype prediction stem primarily from data quality limitations rather than algorithmic inadequacy.
We provide a practical framework for building generalizable models through rigorous cross-dataset evaluation, feature stability assessment, and sample quality diagnostics which are applicable even when mechanistic knowledge is unavailable.
  }
\end{abstract}
