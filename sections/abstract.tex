\begin{abstract}
{
\noindent
Predicting microbial carbon utilisation from genomic data remains challenging despite well-characterised metabolic pathways.
Machine learning models aimed at genotype-phenotype prediction often struggle to generalise beyond their training datasets.
Here, we investigated this generalisation problem using microbial carbon utilisation as a model system, compiling binary growth phenotypes for 819 bacterial genomes across 242 carbon sources from four independent experimental datasets.
Across cross-dataset evaluations, performance improved more from filtering incoherent samples than from changing the modelling setup, suggesting that data coherence was a major limitation.
To address this, we stratified samples based on concordance between a mechanistic predictor (GapMind pathway completeness) and experimental growth outcomes.
Models trained exclusively on concordant samples achieved robust cross-dataset generalisation and consistently recovered known pathway genes as top predictive features.
These models also correctly predicted a subset of samples misclassified by the mechanistic predictor, indicating that machine learning captures information beyond curated pathway rules.
For a well-characterised phenotype (histidine utilisation), as few as 50--100 high-quality training samples sufficed for robust generalisation.
These results indicate that cross-dataset generalisation in this setting depends strongly on the coherence of the training data.
}
\end{abstract}

\keywords{Machine learning, Microbial phenotypes, Genotype-phenotype mapping, Carbon utilisation}
