\noindent

Microbial carbon utilisation is a foundational phenotype for ecology and biotechnology, yet predicting it from genomic data remains challenging despite well-characterised metabolic pathways.
Machine learning models often achieve high within-dataset accuracy yet fail to generalise across datasets, and whether this reflects algorithmic limits or the training data itself has remained unresolved.
Using microbial carbon utilisation as a model system, we integrated published binary growth phenotypes for 819 bacterial genomes across 242 carbon sources from four independent experimental datasets and found that data coherence was a major determinant of generalisation: training data with internally inconsistent genotype--phenotype relationships drove models to learn dataset- or phylogeny-correlated shortcuts.
Stratifying samples by concordance between a mechanistic predictor and experimental outcomes, and training exclusively on concordant samples, substantially improved cross-dataset generalisation within the known-mechanism domain and consistently surfaced known pathway genes as top predictors.
On held-out data of natural composition, these specialist models retained intermediate accuracy and correctly classified some samples misclassified by the mechanistic predictor, showing that machine learning captured transferable signal beyond the curated rules used to define concordance.
Model confidence provided a label-free ranking signal for selective prediction, and prioritising the least-confident genomes for additional measurement improved cross-dataset accuracy more than random sampling, with the largest gains for the weakest phenotypes.
Together these results reframe generalisation in biological machine learning as a problem of data coherence and applicability-domain definition, alongside data volume and algorithm choice: when genotype--phenotype mappings are internally consistent and represented in the available features, models learn transferable signals that can extend beyond curated mechanistic rules; otherwise they default to spurious correlations that fail to generalise.
}
\end{abstract}

\keywords{Machine learning, Microbial phenotypes, Genotype-phenotype mapping, Carbon utilisation}
