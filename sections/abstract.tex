\begin{abstract}
{
\noindent
Machine learning models for predicting microbial phenotypes from genomes routinely achieve high within-dataset accuracy yet fail to generalise across experimental contexts or phylogenetic lineages.
Whether this failure reflects algorithmic limitations or properties of the training data has remained unresolved.
We investigated this question using microbial carbon utilisation as a model system, compiling binary growth phenotypes for 819 bacterial genomes across 242 carbon sources from four independent experimental datasets.
Generalisation failures arose primarily from data coherence rather than algorithmic limitations: when training data contained internally inconsistent genotype--phenotype relationships, models achieved high within-dataset performance but transferred poorly across datasets and phylogenetic contexts, instead learning dataset- or phylogeny-correlated shortcuts.
To improve transferability, we stratified samples by concordance between a mechanistic predictor of carbon utilisation, GapMind pathway completeness, and experimental growth outcomes.
Models trained on concordant samples, where genotype--phenotype relationships were internally consistent and represented in the genomic feature space, achieved robust cross-dataset generalisation and consistently recovered known pathway genes as top predictive features.
On natural-composition held-out data, these models retained intermediate accuracy (median balanced accuracy 0.67), indicating partial recovery of signal beyond the curated pathway rules.
For most of the 15 shared phenotypes, 50--100 high-quality samples were sufficient for near-saturation.
Together, these results reframe ML generalisation as a data coherence problem: when mappings are internally consistent and biologically encoded in the available features, models learn transferable mechanistic signals; when they are not, they default to spurious correlations that fail to generalise.
}
\end{abstract}

\keywords{Machine learning, Microbial phenotypes, Genotype-phenotype mapping, Carbon utilisation}
