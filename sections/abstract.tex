\begin{abstract}
{
\noindent
Microbial carbon utilisation is a foundational phenotype for ecology and biotechnology, yet predicting it from genomic data remains challenging despite well-characterised metabolic pathways.
Machine learning models often achieve high within-dataset accuracy yet fail to generalise across datasets, and whether this reflects algorithmic limits or the training data itself has remained unresolved.
Using microbial carbon utilisation as a model system, we compiled binary growth phenotypes for 819 bacterial genomes across 242 carbon sources from four independent experimental datasets and found that generalisation failures arose primarily from data coherence: training data with internally inconsistent genotype--phenotype relationships drove models to learn dataset- or phylogeny-correlated shortcuts.
Stratifying samples by concordance between a mechanistic predictor and experimental outcomes, and training exclusively on concordant samples, restored cross-dataset generalisation and consistently surfaced known pathway genes as top predictors.
On held-out data of natural composition the models retained intermediate accuracy, capturing signal beyond the curated rules used to define concordance.
Model confidence together with agreement between the model and the mechanistic predictor identified, from genome content alone, which predictions could be trusted, and prioritised the remainder for experimental characterisation of mechanisms beyond the curated pathway.
Together these results reframe generalisation in biological machine learning as a problem of data coherence rather than data volume or algorithm choice: when genotype--phenotype mappings are internally consistent and represented in the available features, models learn transferable mechanistic signals; otherwise they default to spurious correlations that fail to generalise.
}
\end{abstract}

\keywords{Machine learning, Microbial phenotypes, Genotype-phenotype mapping, Carbon utilisation}
