\begin{abstract}
{
\noindent
Microbial carbon utilisation is a foundational phenotype for ecology and biotechnology, yet predicting it from genomic data remains challenging despite well-characterised metabolic pathways.
Machine learning models often achieve high within-dataset accuracy yet fail to generalise across datasets, and whether this reflects algorithmic limits or the training data itself has remained unresolved.
To distinguish these explanations, we integrated binary growth phenotypes for 819 bacterial genomes across 242 carbon sources from four datasets, finding that data coherence was a major determinant of generalisation: inconsistent genotype--phenotype labels drove models to learn dataset- or phylogeny-correlated shortcuts.
When we restricted training to samples agreeing with a mechanistic predictor, the resulting models acted as bounded specialists, recovering known pathway genes as top predictors and rescuing mechanistic false negatives at twice the rate of false positives, though this advantage averaged out on unstratified tests.
Because mechanistic predictors are unavailable for most traits, we tested a simpler filter combining phylogenetic neighbour agreement and experimental-label consistency, which recovered most of the gain without mechanism-specific information.
For deployment, prediction confidence ranked correct above incorrect predictions and defined a practical applicability domain; prioritising low-confidence genomes for measurement improved cross-dataset accuracy more than random or diversity-based sampling, with the largest gains for the lowest-performing phenotypes.
Together, these findings recast generalisation in biological machine learning as a problem of data coherence and applicability-domain definition, alongside data volume and algorithm choice, and suggest a deployment workflow of coherent training, confidence-based applicability detection, and confidence-guided experimentation.
}
\end{abstract}

\keywords{Machine learning, Microbial phenotypes, Genotype-phenotype mapping, Carbon utilisation}
