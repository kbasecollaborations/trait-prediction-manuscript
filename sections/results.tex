\section*{Results}

\subsection*{Core Story Arc}

We expected predicting carbon utilization to be straightforward given well-characterized pathways $\rightarrow$ ML models achieve high within-dataset accuracy but fail to generalize $\rightarrow$ Diagnosis reveals models learn spurious correlations when trained on mixed-quality data $\rightarrow$ Restricting to mechanistically concordant samples enables robust generalization $\rightarrow$ Characterizing discordances reveals the nature of data quality issues $\rightarrow$ Practical framework for building generalizable models without requiring mechanistic knowledge.

\subsection*{Additional Analyses Needed}

Based on this structure, here are the specific analyses required for the discordance characterization (Figure~\ref{fig:discordance_anatomy}):

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Concordance rate calculation:} For each phenotype, calculate the fraction of samples where GapMind prediction matches experimental outcome
\item \textbf{Directional bias quantification:} For discordant samples, classify as GapMind+/Exp-- versus GapMind--/Exp+ and calculate proportions per phenotype
\item \textbf{Dataset-specific patterns:} Calculate concordance rates separately for each dataset to identify whether certain datasets have systematically higher/lower quality
\item \textbf{Prediction of discordant samples:} You mentioned BACC of 0.18--0.4. Can you break this down by:
\begin{itemize}
\item Direction of discordance (are GapMind--/Exp+ samples more predictable than GapMind+/Exp--?)
\item Phenotype (are discordances for some carbon sources more predictable?)
\item This stratification could reveal whether annotation gaps (predictable) versus regulation/measurement error (unpredictable) dominate for different phenotypes
\end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Phylogenetic clustering of discordances:} Do discordant samples cluster in specific clades? This would suggest lineage-specific alternative pathways or systematic annotation gaps in certain taxonomic groups
\end{enumerate}
