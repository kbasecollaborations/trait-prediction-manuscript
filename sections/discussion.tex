\section*{Discussion}

Predicting microbial phenotypes from genomic data underpins ecological characterisation, the study of uncultured taxa, and various biotechnological applications.
However, machine learning models trained on genomic features generalise poorly across datasets~\cite{Pasolli2016MetaAnalysis,Whalen2022Pitfalls}, a limitation typically attributed to limited sample size or feature-space artefacts~\cite{Li2023-up,Yu2025BiasedSampling,Leek2010BatchEffects}.
By integrating four binary growth datasets covering 819 bacterial genomes across 242 carbon sources and training phenotype-specific classifiers using KOFAM features, we identify label--mechanism agreement in the training set as a third axis of generalisation alongside sample size and feature-space effects, manifest most clearly in cross-dataset recall and feature stability.
Concordance-trained predictors behaved as bounded specialists rather than universal models, and their own prediction confidence (although modestly ranked and uncalibrated under cross-dataset shift) ranked correct above incorrect predictions and, for the lowest-performing phenotypes, guided which additional measurements most improved transfer.
These results reframe genome-based phenotype prediction as a problem of training-set curation and applicability-domain definition~\cite{Sambasivan2021DataCascades,Zha2025DataCentric}, alongside the data-volume and algorithm-choice axes that have dominated the field.

\subsection*{Label--Mechanism Agreement Governs Cross-Dataset Transfer}

Poor cross-dataset performance was generally observed when mixed-quality training samples drove models to fit dataset-specific correlations that do not transfer; phylogeny alone explained little of the failure, with in-clade and regular cross-dataset accuracies nearly identical (Figure~\ref{fig:ml_performance}C), placing the source of generalisation loss outside clade structure. Restricting training to samples where experimental labels agreed with mechanistic predictions improved cross-dataset recall and feature stability across datasets.
This shifts the diagnostic question from ``how many samples'' to ``which training subset'', indicating that data curation and applicability-domain definition can be as important as additional training samples~\cite{Sambasivan2021DataCascades,Zha2025DataCentric,Whang2023DataQuality}.

\subsection*{Concordance-Trained Models Are Bounded Specialists}

Choosing concordant samples does more than improve performance: it implicitly defines an applicability domain, and the asymmetric way the concordant-trained model handles discordant samples reveals which kinds of cases lie inside versus outside that domain.
The wide phenotype-level range in cross-dataset balanced accuracy is consistent with multiple coexisting causes of discordance~\cite{Galperin1998Sources,Bernstein2021Uncertainty}: false negatives (GapMind--/Experiment+) may reflect annotation gaps or unknown alternative pathways, whereas false positives (GapMind+/Experiment--) may reflect regulatory repression or experimental artefacts that static genomic features cannot capture.
Consistent with this asymmetry, the concordant-trained model rescued GapMind false negatives at roughly twice the rate of false positives (Figure~\ref{fig:concordant_analysis}D), consistent with annotation-gap false negatives carrying genomic content that the broader KOFAM feature set can recover even when GapMind's curated pathway-completeness rule misses it, while false positives driven by regulation or experimental conditions leave no genomic signature for the model to recover.
Because the rescue rate is non-zero, concordance filtering necessarily discards some discordant samples that carried transferable genomic signal, an independent tradeoff between coverage and mechanistic clarity in the training data.

Previous work concluded that overcoming phylogenetic confounding requires massive datasets ($>$1000 genomes)~\cite{Li2023-up,Yu2025BiasedSampling}.
In our data, feature stability roughly doubled under concordance filtering (Figure~\ref{fig:concordant_analysis}B); models converged on the same predictors across datasets, consistent with concordant samples overrepresenting organisms using conserved, well-annotated pathways~\cite{Martiny2013-ob}.
Consistent with this conserved-pathway interpretation, adding mixed-quality samples was less helpful than selecting samples with strong label--mechanism agreement~\cite{Zha2025DataCentric,Whang2023DataQuality}, and phenotype-filtered GapMind features outperformed comprehensive multi-annotation feature sets (Figure~\ref{fig:improvement_strategies}D), whose limited cross-dataset overlap reflects dataset-specific biases~\cite{Leek2010BatchEffects}.
The concordant-trained model is consequently a specialist that recovers known mechanisms reliably (Figure~\ref{fig:concordant_analysis}B; Table~\ref{tab:main_feature_comparison}) and retains bounded but non-trivial ability to classify discordant cases, rescuing roughly 40\% of GapMind false negatives whose mechanisms leave transferable genomic signatures.
Its advantage over GapMind was visible under random holdout and as the partial rescue of GapMind false negatives in the discordant subset, but averaged out across the unstratified cross-dataset test (Figure~\ref{fig:concordant_analysis}C), supporting use of the concordant specialist where applicability-domain signals are available.

\subsection*{Practical Deployment and Confidence-Guided Experimentation}

Deploying a concordant specialist raises two practical questions: whether a mechanism-free filter can substitute when GapMind is unavailable, and how much labelled training data the specialist requires.
Random holdout overestimates generalisation relative to cross-dataset evaluation~\cite{Roberts2017CrossValidation}, so both must be answered on cross-dataset tests.
When mechanistic predictors such as GapMind are unavailable, a mechanism-free filter combining phylogenetic neighbour agreement and experimental-label consistency reached cross-dataset balanced accuracy indistinguishable from concordance training (Figure~\ref{fig:improvement_strategies})~\cite{Northcutt2021Confident,Bernhardt2022LabelCleaning}, with the benefit arising from cross-source consistency rather than from GapMind specifically.
Despite this balanced-accuracy equivalence, the two filters diverge where replacing GapMind matters most: concordance training systematically improved recall over GapMind across phenotypes (Figure~\ref{fig:improvement_strategies}C), addressing the false-negative gap left by GapMind's conservative pathway-completeness rule, whereas the mechanism-free filter did not.
This contrast suggests that the mechanistic agreement encoded by concordance carries information beyond the cross-source label consistency captured by the mechanism-free filter.
The specialist's performance plateaued with only a few dozen curated training samples per phenotype (Supplementary Figures~\ref{fig:sample_size_histidine} and~\ref{fig:all_phenotype_heatmap}), so raw sample volume ceases to limit cross-dataset transfer past this point and label--mechanism agreement contributes alongside scale; emerging high-throughput phenotyping methods~\cite{Nichols2011Landscape,Gralka2023-ft,Huang2023-lh,Kehe2019-vr} will accelerate progress only if quality is prioritised alongside quantity.

Even with quality-curated training data, deploying any of these specialists on a new genome whose concordance is not known in advance still requires a per-prediction trust signal.
Prediction confidence ranked correct above incorrect predictions, although the score was not calibrated and was overconfident under cross-dataset shift (Figure~\ref{fig:selective_prediction}A,B)~\cite{Weaver2008AppDomain,Geifman2017SelectiveClassification,Hendrickx2024Reject}.
A retrospective experiment-prioritisation simulation showed that selecting the least-confident genomes for additional labelling improved cross-dataset transfer more than random selection, with the largest gain for glucose, the lowest performer among the three phenotypes examined (Figure~\ref{fig:selective_prediction}D).
However, this prediction-level signal does not extend to the phenotype level: no label-free metric we tested correlated with per-phenotype cross-dataset accuracy, suggesting that phenotype difficulty reflects whether the genotype--phenotype mapping is mechanistically learnable rather than the model's own uncertainty.
Cross-dataset feature instability (Figure~\ref{fig:misclassification_diagnosis}C) is a plausible a priori indicator that future evaluation should test directly.

\subsection*{Limitations and Future Directions}

These deployment results rest on several assumptions.
Concordance-based training requires mechanistic tools such as GapMind, which exist only for well-characterised metabolic pathways; the mechanism-free filter offers a partial alternative on balanced accuracy but does not reproduce concordance's recall advantage over GapMind, and still depends on experimental labels from multiple sources. We did not test extension to quantitative traits.
Concordance filtering also extends the coverage--clarity tradeoff noted earlier to a taxonomic dimension: it favours pathways represented in the annotation space and organisms using broadly conserved enzymes, excluding those employing alternative enzymes, divergent pathway variants, or complex regulatory strategies~\cite{Soucy2015HGT,Glasner2020Promiscuity,Gorke2008CCR}, and may introduce taxonomic bias toward well-studied genera, although the design decouples the feature space from the rule used to define concordance (Supplementary Text~S5).
All analyses rely on computational annotations, which carry systematic biases across taxa~\cite{Karlsen2023Genotype,Schnoes2009Annotation,Lobb2020AnnotationCoverage} and may produce lower apparent concordance in poorly studied lineages because of annotation gaps rather than different biology.
We did not attempt to disentangle annotation gaps, alternative pathways, regulatory activity, and phylogenetic accident as candidate causes of discordance; few organisms per novel mechanism would make such separation difficult from genome content alone.
Capturing mechanisms beyond curated rules will therefore require external data such as transcriptomic or regulatory profiles~\cite{Arikan2023MultiOmics}, comparative genomics across closely related strains, and targeted experimental characterisation of out-of-domain organisms; the low-confidence ranking of Figure~\ref{fig:selective_prediction} provides a natural starting point for selecting candidates.

In summary, models of microbial phenotype transferred best when training labels agreed with mechanistic predictions and the feature space matched known pathway biology, systematically surpassing the curated baseline's recall by classifying discordant samples with repeatable genomic signatures, consistent with our framing of label--mechanism agreement as a third axis of generalisation alongside sample volume and feature-space choice.
Genome-only predictors nevertheless face a feature-resolution gap relative to curated annotations, where broader feature sets cannot fully substitute for hand-curated pathway rules and scale alone cannot recover unrepresented signal.
Reliable prediction of microbial phenotypes will therefore require curating training data with strong label--mechanism agreement within appropriately matched feature spaces, using applicability-domain signals to flag when models may extend beyond curated rules, extending the mechanism-free filter towards alternatives such as ensemble disagreement or prediction-confidence-based filtering~\cite{Northcutt2021Confident} that do not require curated experimental labels, and integrating transcriptomic or regulatory data~\cite{Arikan2023MultiOmics} to capture context-dependence that static genomic features cannot.
