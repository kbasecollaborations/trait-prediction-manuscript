\section*{Discussion}

\subsection*{Data Coherence Limits Model Transfer}

% Summary of findings
In our cross-dataset analyses, poor generalisation was not explained by the learning algorithm alone; performance improved mainly when training was restricted to samples with coherent genotype--phenotype relationships.
The resulting models were not universal predictors, but specialists whose reliability could be estimated using confidence and agreement with a mechanistic call.
These specialist models nevertheless retained partial predictive value on discordant samples, showing that ML can capture genome-encoded signal beyond curated pathway rules when that signal is shared across datasets.
GapMind--experiment discordance likely reflects incomplete representation of known mechanisms, or biology not captured by genome-derived features such as regulation or environmental context.
When such samples dominate the training set, models fit correlations that hold within a dataset or clade but do not transfer, so data curation and applicability-domain definition are larger constraints than further algorithmic tuning~\cite{Mey2021Improving}.

% What constitutes high-quality data
We treated samples as high quality when mechanistic predictions and measured growth agreed, because those samples are more likely to reflect pathways represented in the annotation space and to involve organisms using broadly conserved, well-characterised pathways, so filtering favours features that transfer across organisms rather than clade-specific correlates.
For any given carbon source, however, a proportion of organisms employ alternative enzymes, divergent pathway variants, or complex regulatory strategies~\cite{Soucy2015HGT,Glasner2020Promiscuity}; when such organisms are in training, the model lacks mechanistic signal and relies on phylogenetic or dataset-specific correlates that do not generalise.
Data quality also extends beyond mechanistic concordance to genome completeness and annotation consistency~\cite{Bowers2017MIMAG,Parks2015CheckM}, measurement reproducibility, and standardised phenotype calling, so discordance spans both recoverable cases such as annotation gaps and cases in which the relevant mechanism is not represented.

\subsection*{Discordance Defines the Applicability Domain}

% Interpreting discordances
Concordant-trained models reached low cross-dataset balanced accuracy on discordant samples (median 0.34; range 0.14--0.68), which argues against a single explanation for discordance~\cite{Galperin1998Sources,Snitkin2008Model}.
Although these interpretations remain speculative without experimental validation, false negatives (GapMind--/Experiment+) may reflect annotation gaps or unknown alternative pathways, whereas false positives (GapMind+/Experiment--) may reflect regulatory repression or experimental artefacts that static genomic features cannot capture.
Dataset-specific discordance patterns may further reflect differences in experimental protocols, measurement sensitivity, or community composition; disentangling these contributions would require controlled cross-laboratory comparisons, but the variation already motivates protocol comparisons, phenotype-calling checks, and clade-specific error analysis.

% Relationship to previous work and specialisation versus coverage
Previous work has demonstrated that ML models learn phylogenetic signals, concluding that massive datasets ($>$1000 genomes) are needed to overcome confounding~\cite{Li2023-up}.
In our data, phylogenetic confounding was strongest when training mixed concordant and discordant samples and weaker after filtering, likely because concordant samples capture conserved pathways shared across lineages rather than clade-specific patterns~\cite{Collins2018GWAS,Yu2025BiasedSampling}.
Sample size and data quality therefore interact: adding mixed-quality samples was less helpful than selecting samples with coherent mechanistic signal~\cite{Martiny2013-ob,Martiny2015-ie}, and most phenotypes saturated with 50--100 coherent training samples.
Pathway-linked features paired with concordance filtering also transferred better than comprehensive feature sets, whose limited overlap reflects dataset-specific biases~\cite{Leek2010BatchEffects}.
A concordant-trained model is consequently a specialist: filtering enriches the training set for known-mechanism organisms~\cite{Zadrozny2004SelectionBias}, recovering known mechanisms reliably (Figure~\ref{fig:concordant_analysis}B; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:feature_comparison_concordant}) while retaining limited ability to classify discordant cases when their mechanisms leave transferable genomic signatures.
This bound is not escaped by an unfiltered model: novel mechanisms typically span only a few organisms within a single lineage, so a full-data model memorises them as phylogenetic correlates and apparent performance collapses under cross-dataset evaluation.
The practical choice is therefore between a model that is honestly bounded and one whose breadth is an artefact of shortcut learning; the concordant specialist with its applicability-domain detector (Figure~\ref{fig:selective_prediction}) is the deployable predictor, whereas the full-data model is mainly diagnostic.

\subsection*{Implications for Phenotype Prediction}

% Practical implications
Changing the training set and feature representation mattered more than changing the classifier~\cite{Zha2025DataCentric,Whang2023DataQuality,Sambasivan2021DataCascades}.
When mechanistic predictors such as GapMind are available, concordant samples provide the highest-quality training data; otherwise, measurement-confidence filtering and removal of systematically problematic samples offer practical alternatives~\cite{Bernhardt2022LabelCleaning}, and feature stability across independent training sets helps distinguish mechanistic from spurious learning.
Cross-dataset and out-of-clade evaluation provide realistic performance estimates~\cite{Roberts2017CrossValidation}, whereas random holdout evaluation within the aggregated dataset substantially overestimates generalisation.
For deployment on genomes whose concordance cannot be established in advance, prediction confidence and agreement with the mechanistic predictor provide label-free reliability signals: for phenotypes whose models leave room for selective improvement, abstaining on the least-confident predictions within each predicted class raised balanced accuracy substantially (e.g., from 0.76 to 0.90 between full and top-10\% coverage for histidine), and predictions on which GapMind and the model disagreed were close to random (0.49; Figure~\ref{fig:selective_prediction}).
These signals define a practical applicability domain for genome-based phenotype prediction~\cite{Weaver2008AppDomain,Papangelou2025Conformal,Hendrickx2024Reject}.
They also provide experimental-design guidance: high-confidence, GapMind--ML-agreeing predictions can be used conservatively, whereas low-confidence or disagreeing cases are the most informative candidates for targeted phenotyping or mechanistic follow-up.
The retrospective active-learning pilot supports this interpretation: GapMind--ML-disagreement-guided selection improved transfer more than random selection for glucose, the high-shortcut-gap phenotype, whereas little advantage was observed for m-inositol, where the model already generalised.
At the phenotype level, the full-data model's shortcut gap together with the cross-dataset feature instability in Figure~\ref{fig:misclassification_diagnosis}C provides an a priori heuristic for when concordance filtering is likely to pay off (Figure~\ref{fig:selective_prediction}C); when test conditions resemble training conditions, full-dataset training may instead be preferred.
Sample-size requirements were modest across the 15 carbon-utilisation phenotypes analysed here (Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:all_phenotype_heatmap}), pointing to coherent labels rather than raw scale as the binding constraint, and emerging high-throughput phenotyping methods~\cite{Bochner2001PM,Nichols2011Landscape,Gralka2023-ft,Huang2023-lh,Kehe2019-vr} will accelerate data collection only if quality is prioritised alongside quantity.

The reliability signals above are also useful because capturing additional mechanisms from the full training set requires separating signal from noise on the discordant subset, and genome-only data can do this only when the missing mechanism has a repeatable feature signature.
A feature that predicts growth in a discordant organism may reflect (i) an annotation gap, (ii) a genuine alternative pathway, (iii) regulatory or context-dependent activity invisible to static gene content, or (iv) a phylogenetic accident; these causes are not separable from genome content alone, and with only a handful of organisms per novel mechanism they cannot be learned as transferable rules.
Breaking this impasse requires external data: transcriptomic or regulatory profiles to capture context-dependent activity~\cite{Arikan2023MultiOmics}, comparative genomics across closely related strains to separate mechanism from phylogeny, and targeted experimental characterisation of out-of-domain organisms, for which the applicability-domain detector of Figure~\ref{fig:selective_prediction} provides a natural priority list.

\subsection*{Limitations and Future Directions}

% Limitations
The filtering approach requires mechanistic tools such as GapMind, which exist only for well-characterised metabolic pathways; extending it to traits lacking comparable predictors (e.g., stress tolerance, virulence) will require alternative quality-assessment strategies, and our binary growth/no-growth setting may not transfer directly to quantitative traits.
By selecting samples whose phenotypes match the known pathway, we may exclude organisms using alternative routes, and the partial predictability of discordant samples suggests this is a real limitation.
Concordance filtering may further introduce taxonomic bias toward well-studied genera with more complete annotations, so part of the cross-dataset gain could reflect broader taxonomic representation rather than purely mechanistic transferability (Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:taxonomic_bias}).
The design mitigates one related concern: although GapMind-defined concordance could in principle bias retained samples toward the canonical pathways encoded by the features, the concordant-trained models used KOFAM annotations as inputs while retaining the GapMind criterion for sample selection alone, decoupling the feature space from the rule set used to define concordance, and retained intermediate accuracy on the natural-composition held-out test set (Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:full_held_out_test}), classifying samples that the mechanistic predictor itself misclassifies.
Finally, all analyses rely on computational annotations, which carry systematic biases across taxa~\cite{Karlsen2023Genotype,Schnoes2009Annotation}; poorly studied lineages may show lower concordance because of annotation gaps rather than different biology, and zero-imputation feature alignment conflates true gene absence with cross-pipeline annotation mismatch.

% Conclusion
Models transferred best when training data were mechanistically coherent and the feature space matched known pathway biology, and they sometimes exceeded the curated mechanistic baseline by classifying discordant samples with repeatable genomic signatures.
However, genome-only models still face a ceiling set by what current annotations encode: where phenotypes depend on poorly characterised enzymes, transporters, or regulatory programs, scale alone cannot recover signal that is not represented.
Future work should test whether concordance-based filtering generalises to other trait classes (antibiotic resistance, environmental tolerance, host interactions) with comparable mechanistic predictors; develop label-free alternatives such as ensemble disagreement or prediction-confidence filtering for traits without them~\cite{Northcutt2021Confident}; and integrate transcriptomic or regulatory data~\cite{Arikan2023MultiOmics} to capture the context-dependence that static features miss, prioritising clades of elevated discordance for experimental follow-up as candidate sources of novel metabolic strategies.
Together, these findings argue that progress in genotype--phenotype prediction will depend less on larger datasets or more elaborate models than on infrastructure that exposes, curates, and conditions on coherent mappings within appropriately matched feature spaces, while using applicability-domain signals to identify when ML can safely extend beyond curated mechanistic rules.
