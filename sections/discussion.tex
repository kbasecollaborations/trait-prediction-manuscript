\section*{Discussion}

\subsection*{Data Coherence Limits Model Transfer}

% Summary of findings
In our cross-dataset analyses, poor generalisation was not explained by the learning algorithm alone; performance improved substantially when training was restricted to samples with coherent genotype--phenotype relationships.
The resulting models were not universal predictors but bounded specialists, for which the model's own confidence ranked predictions by expected reliability and defined a practical applicability domain.
When mixed-quality samples dominate the training set, models fit correlations that hold within a dataset or clade but do not transfer, so data curation and applicability-domain definition can be as important as further algorithmic tuning~\cite{Sambasivan2021DataCascades,Zha2025DataCentric,Whang2023DataQuality}.

\subsection*{Discordance Defines the Applicability Domain}

% Interpreting discordances
Beyond input-data quality, concordant-trained model behaviour on discordant samples sharpens what ``inside'' and ``outside'' the applicability domain mean in practice.
Low cross-dataset balanced accuracy with a wide phenotype-level range argues against a single explanation for discordance~\cite{Galperin1998Sources,Bernstein2021Uncertainty}.
False negatives (GapMind--/Experiment+) may reflect annotation gaps or unknown alternative pathways, whereas false positives (GapMind+/Experiment--) may reflect regulatory repression or experimental artefacts that static genomic features cannot capture.
Consistent with the false-negative/false-positive asymmetry, the concordant-trained model rescued GapMind false negatives at roughly twice the rate of false positives (Figure~\ref{fig:concordant_analysis}D).

% Relationship to previous work and specialisation versus coverage
Previous work reported that ML models learn phylogenetic signals, concluding that massive datasets ($>$1000 genomes) are needed to overcome confounding~\cite{Li2023-up,Yu2025BiasedSampling}.
In our data, phylogenetic confounding was strongest when training mixed concordant and discordant samples and weaker after filtering, likely because concordant samples capture conserved pathways shared across lineages rather than clade-specific patterns~\cite{Martiny2013-ob}.
Sample size and data quality therefore interact: adding mixed-quality samples was less helpful than selecting samples with coherent mechanistic signal~\cite{Zha2025DataCentric,Whang2023DataQuality}, and most phenotypes saturated with 50--100 coherent training samples.
Pathway-linked features paired with concordance filtering also produced higher transfer performance than comprehensive feature sets, whose limited overlap reflects dataset-specific biases~\cite{Leek2010BatchEffects}.
A concordant-trained model is consequently a specialist: filtering enriches the training set for known-mechanism organisms, recovering known mechanisms reliably (Figure~\ref{fig:concordant_analysis}B; Table~\ref{tab:main_feature_comparison}; Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:feature_comparison} and~\ref{tab:feature_comparison_concordant}) while retaining limited ability to classify discordant cases when their mechanisms leave transferable genomic signatures.
On the natural-composition cross-dataset test set, the specialist's mean balanced accuracy was indistinguishable from GapMind across the 15 phenotypes (Figure~\ref{fig:concordant_analysis}C; failure to reject with $n=15$ paired phenotypes does not establish equivalence, only the absence of a detectable mean shift); the concordant-specialist advantage was visible inside the applicability domain and under random holdout but averaged out across mixed-composition test cells, sharpening rather than weakening the case for an explicit applicability-domain detector.
The deployment choice is therefore between a model with an explicit bounded domain and one whose apparent breadth may partly reflect shortcut learning; the concordant specialist with its confidence-based ranking signal (Figure~\ref{fig:selective_prediction}) is the more deployable predictor, whereas the full-data model is mainly diagnostic.

\subsection*{Implications for Phenotype Prediction}

% Practical implications
Practically, changing the training set and feature representation mattered more than changing the classifier~\cite{Zha2025DataCentric,Whang2023DataQuality}, and random holdout overestimates generalisation relative to cross-dataset evaluation~\cite{Roberts2017CrossValidation}.
When mechanistic predictors such as GapMind are unavailable, a mechanism-free confidence filter that combines only phylogenetic neighbour agreement and experimental-label consistency recovered most of the concordance gain on the full cross-dataset held-out test (cross-dataset balanced accuracy 0.638; Figure~\ref{fig:improvement_strategies})~\cite{Northcutt2021Confident,Bernhardt2022LabelCleaning}, and adding GapMind information to the filter at weights up to 0.5 shifted cross-dataset balanced accuracy by at most 0.015, indicating that the filter benefit arises from cross-source consistency rather than from GapMind specifically.
Removing systematically problematic samples did not measurably improve mean balanced accuracy on its own, and feature stability across independent training sets helps distinguish mechanistic from spurious learning in either regime.
Sample-size requirements were 50--100 concordant samples for most of the 15 carbon-utilisation phenotypes (Supplementary Figures~\ref{fig:sample_size_histidine} and~\ref{fig:all_phenotype_heatmap}), pointing to coherent labels rather than raw scale as the binding constraint; emerging high-throughput phenotyping methods~\cite{Nichols2011Landscape,Gralka2023-ft,Huang2023-lh,Kehe2019-vr} will accelerate data collection only if quality is prioritised alongside quantity.

For deployment on a new genome whose concordance is not known in advance, prediction confidence ranked correct above incorrect predictions, though it was not calibrated and was overconfident under cross-dataset shift (Figure~\ref{fig:selective_prediction}A,B)~\cite{Weaver2008AppDomain,Geifman2017SelectiveClassification,Hendrickx2024Reject}.
A retrospective experiment-prioritisation simulation showed that selecting the least-confident genomes to label improved cross-dataset transfer more than random selection, with the gain concentrated on glucose, the lowest-performing phenotype among the three shown in Figure~\ref{fig:selective_prediction}D, turning the random-sampling sample-size analysis above into a confidence-guided acquisition strategy that reaches the same accuracy with fewer added labels.
Per-phenotype reliability could not be anticipated from confidence alone, since difficulty reflects whether the genotype--phenotype mapping is mechanistically learnable; cross-dataset feature instability (Figure~\ref{fig:misclassification_diagnosis}C) remains an a priori indicator of phenotypes whose generalisation is fragile.

\subsection*{Limitations and Future Directions}

% Limitations
These deployment results rest on several assumptions and limitations.
The strongest filtering setting, concordance-based training, requires mechanistic tools such as GapMind, which exist only for well-characterised metabolic pathways; the mechanism-free confidence filter described above captures most of this gain and offers one such alternative for traits lacking comparable predictors (e.g., stress tolerance, virulence), though it still depends on experimental labels from multiple sources, and our binary growth/no-growth setting may not transfer directly to quantitative traits.
Concordance filtering also favours pathways represented in the annotation space and organisms using broadly conserved enzymes, so retained features transfer across organisms rather than encoding clade-specific correlates; organisms employing alternative enzymes, divergent pathway variants, or complex regulatory strategies~\cite{Soucy2015HGT,Glasner2020Promiscuity,Gorke2008CCR} are excluded by the concordance filter, and the partial predictability of discordant samples shows that concordance filtering removes predictable discordant genotype--phenotype associations.
Concordance filtering may further introduce taxonomic bias toward well-studied genera, since both the annotation database and curated pathway rules were built largely from these lineages.
A portion of the cross-dataset gain we attribute to data coherence may therefore reflect a narrower taxonomic distribution rather than mechanistic coherence, and the present design cannot fully disentangle mechanistic coherence from taxonomic representativeness.
The taxonomic-representativeness confound is most acute for phenotypes whose discordant samples come from poorly annotated clades.
The design mitigates a related circularity concern by decoupling the feature space from the rule used to define concordance (Supplementary Text~S5).
Finally, all analyses rely on computational annotations, which carry systematic biases across taxa~\cite{Karlsen2023Genotype,Schnoes2009Annotation,Lobb2020AnnotationCoverage}; poorly studied lineages may show lower concordance because of annotation gaps rather than different biology, and zero-imputation feature alignment conflates true gene absence with cross-pipeline annotation mismatch.
Capturing mechanisms beyond curated rules will require separating signal from noise on the discordant subset, but with only a handful of organisms per novel mechanism, annotation gaps, alternative pathways, regulatory activity, and phylogenetic accident are not separable from genome content alone.
Breaking this impasse requires external data such as transcriptomic or regulatory profiles~\cite{Arikan2023MultiOmics}, comparative genomics across closely related strains, and targeted experimental characterisation of out-of-domain organisms, for which the applicability-domain detector of Figure~\ref{fig:selective_prediction} provides a natural priority list.

% Conclusion
Models transferred best when training data were mechanistically coherent and the feature space matched known pathway biology, sometimes exceeding the curated baseline by classifying discordant samples with repeatable genomic signatures.
However, genome-only models face a ceiling set by current annotations, where scale alone cannot recover unrepresented signal.
Progress in genotype--phenotype prediction will therefore require curating coherent mappings within appropriately matched feature spaces while using applicability-domain signals to identify when ML can extend beyond curated mechanistic rules.
Future work should test whether concordance-based filtering generalises to other trait classes (antibiotic resistance, environmental tolerance, host interactions), extend the mechanism-free phylogeny and experimental-label confidence filter shown here towards alternatives that do not require curated experimental labels at training time, such as ensemble disagreement or prediction-confidence filtering~\cite{Northcutt2021Confident}, and integrate transcriptomic or regulatory data~\cite{Arikan2023MultiOmics} to capture context-dependence that static features miss, prioritising clades of elevated discordance as candidate sources of novel metabolic strategies.
