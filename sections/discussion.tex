\section*{Discussion}

\subsection*{Data Coherence Limits Model Transfer}

% Summary of findings
In our cross-dataset analyses, poor generalisation was not explained by the learning algorithm alone; performance improved mainly when training was restricted to samples with coherent genotype--phenotype relationships.
The resulting models were not universal predictors but bounded specialists, for which the model's own confidence ranked the more reliable predictions and defined a practical applicability domain.
When mixed-quality samples dominate the training set, models fit correlations that hold within a dataset or clade but do not transfer, so data curation and applicability-domain definition are larger constraints than further algorithmic tuning~\cite{Mey2021Improving}.

% What constitutes high-quality data
Concordance filtering favours pathways represented in the annotation space and organisms using broadly conserved enzymes, so retained features transfer across organisms rather than encoding clade-specific correlates.
For any given carbon source, however, a proportion of organisms employ alternative enzymes, divergent pathway variants, or complex regulatory strategies~\cite{Soucy2015HGT,Glasner2020Promiscuity}; when these are in training, the model lacks mechanistic signal and relies on phylogenetic or dataset-specific correlates that do not generalise.
Data quality also extends beyond mechanistic concordance to genome completeness, annotation consistency, and measurement reproducibility~\cite{Bowers2017MIMAG,Parks2015CheckM} (Supplementary Text~S14).

\subsection*{Discordance Defines the Applicability Domain}

% Interpreting discordances
Concordant-trained models reached low cross-dataset balanced accuracy on discordant samples (median 0.34; range 0.14--0.68), arguing against a single explanation for discordance~\cite{Galperin1998Sources,Snitkin2008Model}.
False negatives (GapMind--/Experiment+) may reflect annotation gaps or unknown alternative pathways, whereas false positives (GapMind+/Experiment--) may reflect regulatory repression or experimental artefacts that static genomic features cannot capture.
Empirically, the model rescued GapMind false negatives at roughly twice the rate of false positives (40\% vs 20\% pooled; paired Wilcoxon $p = 0.02$ across phenotypes; Figure~\ref{fig:concordant_analysis}D), consistent with annotation-gap discordance leaving a residual genomic signature ML can read while regulatory or context-dependent discordance does not.
Dataset-specific patterns also motivate protocol and phenotype-calling comparisons.

% Relationship to previous work and specialisation versus coverage
Previous work has demonstrated that ML models learn phylogenetic signals, concluding that massive datasets ($>$1000 genomes) are needed to overcome confounding~\cite{Li2023-up}.
In our data, phylogenetic confounding was strongest when training mixed concordant and discordant samples and weaker after filtering, likely because concordant samples capture conserved pathways shared across lineages rather than clade-specific patterns~\cite{Collins2018GWAS,Yu2025BiasedSampling}.
Sample size and data quality therefore interact: adding mixed-quality samples was less helpful than selecting samples with coherent mechanistic signal~\cite{Martiny2013-ob,Martiny2015-ie}, and most phenotypes saturated with 50--100 coherent training samples.
Pathway-linked features paired with concordance filtering also transferred better than comprehensive feature sets, whose limited overlap reflects dataset-specific biases~\cite{Leek2010BatchEffects}.
A concordant-trained model is consequently a specialist: filtering enriches the training set for known-mechanism organisms~\cite{Zadrozny2004SelectionBias}, recovering known mechanisms reliably (Figure~\ref{fig:concordant_analysis}B; Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:feature_comparison} and~\ref{tab:feature_comparison_concordant}, where 75\% of concordant-trained unique stable cluster representatives for histidine map to the canonical degradation module M00045 against 29\% under full-data training) while retaining limited ability to classify discordant cases when their mechanisms leave transferable genomic signatures.
On the natural-composition cross-dataset test set, the specialist's mean balanced accuracy matched GapMind across the 15 phenotypes (Figure~\ref{fig:concordant_analysis}C, paired Wilcoxon $p = 0.52$); its advantage was visible inside the applicability domain and under random holdout but averaged out across mixed-composition test cells, sharpening rather than weakening the case for an explicit applicability-domain detector.
The practical choice is therefore between a model that is honestly bounded and one whose breadth is an artefact of shortcut learning; the concordant specialist with its applicability-domain detector (Figure~\ref{fig:selective_prediction}) is the deployable predictor, whereas the full-data model is mainly diagnostic.

\subsection*{Implications for Phenotype Prediction}

% Practical implications
Changing the training set and feature representation mattered more than changing the classifier~\cite{Zha2025DataCentric,Whang2023DataQuality,Sambasivan2021DataCascades}.
When mechanistic predictors such as GapMind are available, concordant samples provide the highest-quality training data; otherwise, measurement-confidence filtering and removal of systematically problematic samples offer practical alternatives~\cite{Bernhardt2022LabelCleaning}, and feature stability across independent training sets helps distinguish mechanistic from spurious learning.
Random holdout overestimates generalisation relative to cross-dataset evaluation~\cite{Roberts2017CrossValidation}.
For genomes whose concordance is not known in advance, prediction confidence ($\max(p,\,1-p)$) provided a label-free reliability signal: abstaining on the least-confident predictions raised balanced accuracy for phenotypes the model already predicts well (histidine, from 0.76 to 0.84 between full and half coverage), although both models were overconfident under cross-dataset shift and confidence ranked rather than calibrated (Figure~\ref{fig:selective_prediction}A,B).
These signals define a practical applicability domain for genome-based phenotype prediction~\cite{Weaver2008AppDomain,Papangelou2025Conformal,Hendrickx2024Reject}.
A retrospective experiment-prioritisation simulation supported this: among label-free strategies, selecting the least-confident genomes to label improved cross-dataset transfer more than random selection, and the gain concentrated on glucose, the weak generaliser (Figure~\ref{fig:selective_prediction}C,D).
We could not, however, anticipate per-phenotype reliability from confidence alone, because a phenotype's difficulty reflects whether its genotype--phenotype mapping is mechanistically learnable rather than the novelty of its genomes.
Cross-dataset feature instability (Figure~\ref{fig:misclassification_diagnosis}C) remains an a priori indicator of phenotypes whose generalisation is fragile.
Sample-size requirements were modest across the 15 carbon-utilisation phenotypes (Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:all_phenotype_heatmap}), pointing to coherent labels rather than raw scale as the binding constraint; emerging high-throughput phenotyping methods~\cite{Bochner2001PM,Nichols2011Landscape,Gralka2023-ft,Huang2023-lh,Kehe2019-vr} will accelerate data collection only if quality is prioritised alongside quantity.

Capturing mechanisms beyond curated rules requires separating signal from noise on the discordant subset, but with only a handful of organisms per novel mechanism, annotation gaps, alternative pathways, regulatory activity, and phylogenetic accident are not separable from genome content alone.
Breaking this impasse requires external data: transcriptomic or regulatory profiles~\cite{Arikan2023MultiOmics}, comparative genomics across closely related strains, and targeted experimental characterisation of out-of-domain organisms --- for which the applicability-domain detector of Figure~\ref{fig:selective_prediction} provides a natural priority list.

\subsection*{Limitations and Future Directions}

% Limitations
The filtering approach requires mechanistic tools such as GapMind, which exist only for well-characterised metabolic pathways; extending it to traits lacking comparable predictors (e.g., stress tolerance, virulence) will require alternative quality-assessment strategies, and our binary growth/no-growth setting may not transfer directly to quantitative traits.
By selecting samples whose phenotypes match the known pathway, we may exclude organisms using alternative routes, and the partial predictability of discordant samples suggests this is a real limitation.
Concordance filtering may further introduce taxonomic bias toward well-studied genera with more complete annotations, so part of the cross-dataset gain could reflect broader taxonomic representation rather than purely mechanistic transferability (Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:taxonomic_bias}).
The design mitigates a related circularity concern by decoupling the feature space from the rule used to define concordance (Supplementary Text~S15).
Finally, all analyses rely on computational annotations, which carry systematic biases across taxa~\cite{Karlsen2023Genotype,Schnoes2009Annotation}; poorly studied lineages may show lower concordance because of annotation gaps rather than different biology, and zero-imputation feature alignment conflates true gene absence with cross-pipeline annotation mismatch.

% Conclusion
Models transferred best when training data were mechanistically coherent and the feature space matched known pathway biology, and they sometimes exceeded the curated mechanistic baseline by classifying discordant samples with repeatable genomic signatures.
However, genome-only models still face a ceiling set by what current annotations encode: where phenotypes depend on poorly characterised enzymes, transporters, or regulatory programs, scale alone cannot recover signal that is not represented.
Future work should test whether concordance-based filtering generalises to other trait classes (antibiotic resistance, environmental tolerance, host interactions) with comparable mechanistic predictors; develop label-free alternatives such as ensemble disagreement or prediction-confidence filtering for traits without them~\cite{Northcutt2021Confident}; and integrate transcriptomic or regulatory data~\cite{Arikan2023MultiOmics} to capture the context-dependence that static features miss, prioritising clades of elevated discordance for experimental follow-up as candidate sources of novel metabolic strategies.
Together, these findings argue that progress in genotype--phenotype prediction will depend less on larger datasets or more elaborate models than on infrastructure that exposes, curates, and conditions on coherent mappings within appropriately matched feature spaces, while using applicability-domain signals to identify when ML can safely extend beyond curated mechanistic rules.
