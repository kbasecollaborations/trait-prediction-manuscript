\section*{Discussion}

Predicting microbial phenotypes from genomic data underpins ecological characterisation, the study of uncultured taxa, and various biotechnological applications. However, machine learning models trained on genomic features generalise poorly across datasets, a limitation typically attributed to limited sample size or feature-space artefacts.
By integrating four binary growth datasets covering 819 bacterial genomes across 242 carbon sources, we trained phenotype-specific binary classifiers using KOFAM features. We found that when training samples mix mechanistically tractable and intractable cases, models fit dataset and clade-specific shortcuts rather than transferable pathway features.
Restricting training to mechanistically concordant samples produced bounded specialist predictors that recovered known pathway genes and rescued mechanistic false negatives at approximately twice the rate of false positives, and a mechanism-free filter combining phylogenetic neighbour agreement and experimental-label consistency recovered most of this gain without mechanism-specific tools.
Per-genome prediction confidence ranked correct above incorrect predictions, defining a practical applicability domain for deployment.

\subsection*{Label--Mechanism Agreement Governs Cross-Dataset Transfer}

Poor cross-dataset performance was not explained by the learning algorithm alone; mixed-quality training samples drove models to fit correlations that hold within a single dataset or clade but do not transfer, and restricting training to samples where experimental labels agreed with mechanistic predictions consistently rescued generalisation.
This shifts the diagnostic question from ``which algorithm'' to ``which training subset'', indicating that data curation and applicability-domain definition can be as important as further algorithmic tuning~\cite{Sambasivan2021DataCascades,Zha2025DataCentric,Whang2023DataQuality}.

\subsection*{Concordance-Trained Models Are Bounded Specialists}

The behaviour of the concordant-trained model on discordant samples sharpens what ``inside'' and ``outside'' the applicability domain mean in practice.
The wide phenotype-level range in cross-dataset balanced accuracy argues against a single explanation for discordance~\cite{Galperin1998Sources,Bernstein2021Uncertainty}: false negatives (GapMind--/Experiment+) may reflect annotation gaps or unknown alternative pathways, whereas false positives (GapMind+/Experiment--) may reflect regulatory repression or experimental artefacts that static genomic features cannot capture.
Consistent with this asymmetry, the concordant-trained model rescued GapMind false negatives at roughly twice the rate of false positives (Figure~\ref{fig:concordant_analysis}D), implying that concordance filtering, when used to define the training set, also excludes some discordant samples whose mechanisms leave transferable genomic signatures and therefore trades a coverage cost for mechanistic clarity.

Previous work concluded that overcoming phylogenetic confounding requires massive datasets ($>$1000 genomes)~\cite{Li2023-up,Yu2025BiasedSampling}.
In our data, phylogenetic confounding was strongest when training mixed concordant and discordant samples and weaker after filtering, likely because concordant samples capture conserved pathways shared across lineages rather than clade-specific patterns~\cite{Martiny2013-ob}.
Adding mixed-quality samples was less helpful than selecting samples with strong label--mechanism agreement~\cite{Zha2025DataCentric,Whang2023DataQuality}, and pathway-linked features paired with concordance filtering outperformed comprehensive feature sets, whose limited overlap reflects dataset-specific biases~\cite{Leek2010BatchEffects}.
The concordant-trained model is consequently a specialist that recovers known mechanisms reliably (Figure~\ref{fig:concordant_analysis}B; Table~\ref{tab:main_feature_comparison}) while retaining limited ability to classify discordant cases whose mechanisms leave transferable genomic signatures.
Its advantage over GapMind was visible inside the applicability domain and under random holdout but averaged out across the unstratified cross-dataset test (Figure~\ref{fig:concordant_analysis}C), positioning the concordant specialist as the more deployable predictor and the full-data model as diagnostic.

\subsection*{Practical Deployment and Confidence-Guided Experimentation}

Practically, changing the training set and feature representation mattered more than changing the classifier~\cite{Zha2025DataCentric,Whang2023DataQuality}, and random holdout overestimates generalisation relative to cross-dataset evaluation~\cite{Roberts2017CrossValidation}.
When mechanistic predictors such as GapMind are unavailable, a confidence filter combining phylogenetic neighbour agreement and experimental-label consistency reached cross-dataset balanced accuracy indistinguishable from concordance training (Figure~\ref{fig:improvement_strategies})~\cite{Northcutt2021Confident,Bernhardt2022LabelCleaning}, with the benefit arising from cross-source consistency rather than from GapMind specifically.
Concordance-trained ML nevertheless exceeded GapMind recall on 12 of 15 phenotypes (Figure~\ref{fig:improvement_strategies}C), addressing the false-negative gap left by GapMind's conservative pathway-completeness rule, whereas the mechanism-free filter did not.
Sample-size saturation at 50--100 concordant samples for most phenotypes (Supplementary Figures~\ref{fig:sample_size_histidine} and~\ref{fig:all_phenotype_heatmap}) points to label--mechanism agreement rather than raw scale as the binding constraint, so emerging high-throughput phenotyping methods~\cite{Nichols2011Landscape,Gralka2023-ft,Huang2023-lh,Kehe2019-vr} will accelerate progress only if quality is prioritised alongside quantity.

For deployment on a new genome whose concordance is not known in advance, prediction confidence ranked correct above incorrect predictions, although the score was not calibrated and was overconfident under cross-dataset shift (Figure~\ref{fig:selective_prediction}A,B)~\cite{Weaver2008AppDomain,Geifman2017SelectiveClassification,Hendrickx2024Reject}.
A retrospective experiment-prioritisation simulation showed that selecting the least-confident genomes for additional labelling improved cross-dataset transfer more than random selection, with the largest gains for the lowest-performing phenotype (Figure~\ref{fig:selective_prediction}D).
Confidence alone cannot anticipate per-phenotype reliability, since phenotype difficulty reflects whether the genotype--phenotype mapping is mechanistically learnable; cross-dataset feature instability (Figure~\ref{fig:misclassification_diagnosis}C) provides a complementary a priori indicator.

\subsection*{Limitations and Future Directions}

These deployment results rest on several assumptions.
Concordance-based training requires mechanistic tools such as GapMind, which exist only for well-characterised metabolic pathways; the mechanism-free confidence filter offers a partial alternative but still depends on experimental labels from multiple sources, and the binary growth setting may not transfer directly to quantitative traits.
Concordance filtering also favours pathways represented in the annotation space and organisms using broadly conserved enzymes, excluding those employing alternative enzymes, divergent pathway variants, or complex regulatory strategies~\cite{Soucy2015HGT,Glasner2020Promiscuity,Gorke2008CCR}, and may introduce taxonomic bias toward well-studied genera, although the design decouples the feature space from the rule used to define concordance (Supplementary Text~S5).
All analyses rely on computational annotations, which carry systematic biases across taxa~\cite{Karlsen2023Genotype,Schnoes2009Annotation,Lobb2020AnnotationCoverage} and may produce lower apparent concordance in poorly studied lineages because of annotation gaps rather than different biology.
With only a handful of organisms per novel mechanism, annotation gaps, alternative pathways, regulatory activity, and phylogenetic accident are not separable from genome content alone.
Capturing mechanisms beyond curated rules will therefore require external data such as transcriptomic or regulatory profiles~\cite{Arikan2023MultiOmics}, comparative genomics across closely related strains, and targeted experimental characterisation of out-of-domain organisms, for which the applicability-domain detector of Figure~\ref{fig:selective_prediction} provides a natural priority list.

In summary, models of microbial phenotype transferred best when training labels agreed with mechanistic predictions and the feature space matched known pathway biology, occasionally surpassing the curated baseline by classifying discordant samples with repeatable genomic signatures.
Genome-only predictors nevertheless face a ceiling set by current annotations, where scale alone cannot recover unrepresented signal.
Reliable prediction of microbial phenotypes will therefore require curating training data with strong label--mechanism agreement within appropriately matched feature spaces, using applicability-domain signals to flag when models may extend beyond curated rules, extending the mechanism-free filter towards alternatives such as ensemble disagreement or prediction-confidence-based filtering~\cite{Northcutt2021Confident} that do not require curated experimental labels, and integrating transcriptomic or regulatory data~\cite{Arikan2023MultiOmics} to capture context-dependence that static genomic features cannot.
